xLamina manual

GPU focus stacker

Quick start

xLamina stacks a folder of focus-bracketed frames into one image that is sharp throughout. It needs 64-bit Windows 10 or 11 and an NVIDIA graphics card (GeForce GTX 16 / RTX 20 series or newer) with a current driver.

  1. Open the frames: Open in the toolbar (Ctrl+O) and browse to the photos. Select one photo to open its whole folder, or drop the folder onto the window.
  2. To stack only some of a folder's files: select several in the same window (Shift or Ctrl + click), or drop those files onto the window. File > Open a whole folder… (Ctrl+Shift+O) chooses a folder by its name instead. Raw files are not listed, so the developed frames are easy to pick; a folder that holds only raw files therefore looks empty. They cannot be stacked: develop them to TIFF or JPEG first.
  3. Tick the outputs you want in the Render panel. B and C are ticked by default.
  4. Press Render (F5). The viewer splits in two while it works and shows the stack being built.
  5. Look at the results: the tabs under the viewer switch between the outputs. Wheel to zoom, drag to pan.
  6. Save as… (Ctrl+S) writes the image on screen. Every output is also written to <folder>_stacker beside the frames.

Frames: what xLamina reads

  • TIFF, 8-bit or 16-bit, RGB or grey, uncompressed or compressed (LZW, ZIP, PackBits), and JPEG. Uncompressed TIFF reads fastest.
  • Camera raw files and DNG are not read. Export TIFFs from a raw converter first.
  • Not read: CMYK, Lab and YCbCr TIFFs, 32-bit (floating-point) TIFFs, and files whose first image is a small preview. Export plain RGB.
  • Every image of the stack's size is a frame, so keep other stackers' results and duplicate exports out of the folder: an image of the same size would be stacked as a frame. Images of another size are listed greyed out and left alone; point at a grey row for the reason.
  • The frames open in the order of their names, numbers read as numbers (frame9 before frame10).
  • At most 512 frames in one render. A folder with more than 500 images is not opened straight away: xLamina asks first and offers Choose files…, so a folder that holds all of your pictures does not get read from end to end.
  • The frames' colour profile (for example Adobe RGB) is kept in the outputs and used for display.
  • Frames smaller than 64 x 64 px are refused.
  • The orientation flag of a camera held upright is not applied: such a stack comes out on its side and can be turned afterwards.

Frames are read once and kept in memory while they fit (up to 60 % of the machine's RAM), so a second render of the same folder starts at once. A fast SSD is enough; a RAM disk gives no extra speed.

The frame list

The list is the stack order. Click a frame to see it at full resolution. A stack opened from several selected files lists only those files.

ColourMeaningWhat to do
RedThe shift from the previous frame is an outlier: the stage or the specimen moved.Render > Drop shifted frames, then Render again.
AmberSoft: the focus energy fell under its neighbours' (blurred during the exposure). The cutoff is 0.90; change it in File > Preferences > General > Soft frame below.Render > Drop soft frames.

The three Drop buttons are also in the toolbar; they untick the flagged frames and leave the files alone. Render > Tick all frames puts every frame back.

Changing the order

  • Drag a row to move it.
  • Render > Sort frames by name or by time taken (the capture time in the file's EXIF; the file's modification time when there is none).
  • Right-click a frame for: Add this frame again, Remove this copy, Move to top, Move to bottom, Reverse the whole order, Untick the frames above / below this one.

Outputs: the stacking methods

Tick one or more in the Render panel. Each ticked output becomes a tab after the render. Start with B and C; the others appear when View > Developer controls is on.

OutputWhat it doesUse it for
BDepth map. Finds one smooth depth per pixel and takes each pixel from the frame at that depth, with soft transitions.The cleanest tones and backgrounds.
CLaplacian pyramid. Takes every detail, at every scale, from the frame where it is strongest.The most detail and overlapping structures. Noisier, and bright highlights can glow.
Cn developerC where the detail is above the noise, B elsewhere.C's detail without its grain in flat areas.
CBn developerCn that also falls back to B where C's choice disagrees with the depth map.Fewer artefacts from frames far from the true depth.
Cx developerCBn with a stricter test: a disputed choice stays only if the next finer scale chose the same frame.Glow around defocused highlights.
Cd developerPyramid with its fine scales locked to B's depth.Glints picked up from other depths.
Cg, Cg2 developerPyramid with its coarsest scales (one more in Cg2) taken from B.Glare from defocused highlights.
H developerBrightness from CBn, colour from B.C's detail with B's colour.
A, B0, CB developerFocus-weighted average; B's raw depth, unsmoothed; CBn without the noise rule. Ticked under Tools > Developer outputs.Diagnosis.
Cn equals C at smoothing 3. With Smoothing (C) at its default of 2 the two differ on about 3 % of pixels, so tick Cn only when C's smoothing is lower.

Render settings

Render > Reset settings to defaults puts the panel back as installed. With Developer controls on, the panel also shows Radius (C), Radius (B) and the Advanced group.

Dust map: removing sensor dust

Dust on the sensor sits at the same pixels of every frame. Once the frames are aligned, each a little differently, one speck becomes a short dark trail in the stack. A dust map removes the specks from every frame before stacking.

  1. Take the dust map: a photograph of an evenly lit white background through the same optics, at the frames' size. Out of focus is fine; it must be bright and even.
  2. File > Set dust map…, or Choose… in the Dust map row of the Render panel, and choose it. The dust map's window opens (below).
  3. The file window lists TIFF, JPEG and PNG files; "Images and raw files" in its file type box lists raw files too. A raw file can be tried, but it is the whole sensor and frames developed from raw are usually cropped a little smaller: develop the dust photograph the same way as the frames and choose that file.
  4. Check what is marked, adjust if needed, and press Use this dust map.
  5. Render as usual. The map stays in use for every stack of that size, also after a restart, until you press × in the Dust map row. Spots… there opens the window again.
  • The map shows where the dust is. How dark each speck is, is measured on the frames of each render, so the map does not have to match the frames' exposure.
  • Only the pixels in and around a speck are changed: they are brightened by what the speck took away, or filled in from their surroundings where almost no light is left. Everything else is untouched.
  • A dust map kept in the stack's folder is not stacked as a frame when "dust map" is in its file name, or when it is the map in use.
  • A map of another size than the frames cannot be used; xLamina asks before rendering without it.
  • Saved file names get _dust while a dust map is in use.
  • With fewer than 8 frames there is too little to measure on, and every speck is filled in from its surroundings.
Dust moves. If trails come back, take a new dust map.

While it renders

Zoom and pan stay put as the frames change, so you can watch one spot through the whole stack. Each live image costs the GPUs a little time; choose Off when you only want the result.

Viewing the results

  • Wheel: zoom about the cursor. Drag: pan. Double-click: fit.
  • Clicking a frame in the list shows that frame; clicking a tab goes back to the output.

Scale bar and measuring

  • Shift while dragging keeps the line level, upright or at 45°.
  • The middle or right button pans while Measure is on; the wheel zooms as always.
  • Backspace removes the last line; View > Clear measurements removes them all. Opening another folder clears them too.
  • Lengths are in the plane of the reference frame. A stack whose scale changes with depth (see the geometry report's µm/px range) is off by that much at its ends.
  • The lines and the bar are drawn on the screen only: they are not saved into the output files.
  • In the Compare window the scale applies to images of the size of this render's outputs: its B and C, the depth map, a sweep's variants. An image of another size (another program's stack, say) has no scale bar and its lengths are in pixels.
  • Measure and Retouch do not run together: turning one on leaves the other.

Saving and the files written

Save as… (Ctrl+S) writes the image on show:

  • 16-bit TIFF with the frames' colour profile,
  • JPEG at 100 % quality, or PNG: the image as displayed (sRGB, 8 bits).

The suggested name carries the render's finish time and settings, for example B_2026-11-08_14-15-27_bicubic_s2_c32_b0_1.49mm.tif: output, date and time, interpolation, smoothing, C radius, B radius, and the output's field of view. _rs or _band is added while a rolling-shutter correction is on.

Every render also writes to the output folder (<folder>_stacker unless you set another):

FileHolds
B.tif, C.tif, …Each ticked output as a 16-bit TIFF.
report.jsonThe geometry report: every frame's transform, the reference frame, the crop, µm/px.
list.txt, stack.txtThe frame list with its transforms and the render's settings, for the command line.

File > Open output folder (Ctrl+Shift+O) opens it in Explorer.

Compare: two images side by side

Press Compare above the viewer (Ctrl+2). The two panes share one pan and zoom, so the same spot is always on show in both.

Difference

Comparison images in the main window

File > Add comparison image… (Ctrl+I), or dropping an image file onto a finished render, adds another stacker's output as a tab beside xLamina's. Close such a tab with the middle mouse button.

Retouch: paint one image into another

Press Retouch above the viewer, or R in the two-up viewer. The left pane becomes the result; the brush paints the right pane's image into it. Typical use: B as the base, with C painted in where B lost detail (or the other way round).

Hold Space to see the source in the result pane. The painting is done on the 16-bit data, not on the screen image.

Batch render

File > Batch render… (Ctrl+Shift+B) renders several folders one after the other with the Render panel's current settings. Dropping several folders onto the window opens the same dialog with them queued.

Each folder's outputs go to <folder>_stacker. A folder that fails or holds no stack is skipped and listed in the log at the end.

Preferences

File > Preferences… (Ctrl+,).

Performance

Hardware settings. They change the speed, never the result.

The System tab lists the xLamina version, the GPUs, the RAM, the program folder and the settings file.

Benchmark

Tools > Benchmark… (Ctrl+B) times the engine under different hardware settings.

ModeWhat it runs
QuickUp to 40 frames at half resolution: the current settings, each GPU alone, and frames streamed instead of resident.
ThoroughThe whole stack with every setting varied in turn, then the best of each combined.
Single runThe whole stack once on the current settings. Each run stays in the table, to compare the machine against itself (a new driver, an overclock).

Geometry report, Log and Hardware

Three panels share the bottom of the window; click a tab to bring one forward. View lists every panel so a closed one can be shown again.

Log holds the engine's messages; when a render fails the reason is here. Tools > Copy log copies it.

Developer controls developer

View > Developer controls (also in Preferences > General), on when xLamina is first installed, shows the tuning controls: the outputs beyond B and C, Radius (C), Radius (B), the Advanced group, Tools > Developer outputs, and the depth map tab. Turning it off changes nothing about a render except that the extra outputs are not rendered.

Advanced (depth and composition)

Change one value at a time and judge at 1:1; a sweep does this for you. Defaults restores them all.

The depth map developer

With Developer controls on, a depth tab sits after the outputs. It shows which frame each pixel of B comes from, in colour. It is a view only; it changes nothing about a render.

Holding a layer and stepping through the layers

  • Click on the depth map: the layer under the cursor is held. The note beside the cursor still reads the frame under the cursor.
  • Arrow keys (click the image first) or PgUp / PgDn: the next layer down or up the stack. Frames the map does not use at all are passed over. With nothing held, the arrow keys start from the frame under the cursor.
  • Esc, or a click on the held layer: let go; the highlight follows the cursor again.
  • While a layer is held, PgUp / PgDn step layers instead of tabs. Dragging still pans, the wheel still zooms.
To check a feature: put the crosshair on it, read the frame name in the note, then click that frame in the list to see the source.

In the two-up viewer

The note beside the cursor works on every image of the render there, so you can read the frame while looking at B or C.

Sweeps: try several settings at once tuning

Compare > Run sweep… (Ctrl+Shift+R) renders B several times, each with one setting changed from the Render panel's, over a single alignment. The two-up viewer then opens with the variants listed by how much they differ from the base, so the first rows are the ones worth looking at.

In the viewer's list the Change column is the share of B's pixels that differ from the base by more than 2 %. Compare > Open sweep folder… (Ctrl+Shift+W) reopens an earlier sweep.

Updates

A few seconds after it starts, xLamina asks GitHub for the latest release. A newer one lights an Update to x.y.z button at the right end of the toolbar. It shows what changed and, on Update and restart, downloads the release, swaps the files in the program's folder and restarts. Settings are kept.

  • Help > Check for updates… asks at any time.
  • Help > Check for updates at start turns the automatic check off or on.

Command line and settings file

The same engine runs without the window:

stackcli run <folder> [<folder2> ...] [--out DIR] [--outputs B,C,Cn] [--fov-mm X] [--scale 0.5] [--interp bicubic] [--smoothing 2]

stackcli with no arguments lists every command and option.

All settings, the window layout, the theme and the recent folders are kept in %APPDATA%\xLamina\xLamina.ini.

When something goes wrong

A render that fails says what happened and what to try next: the graphics card or the PC out of memory, a frame that could not be read (named), an output folder that could not be written, a graphics driver that stopped responding. The box has two buttons: Show the log and Save a report….

  • Help > Report a problem… saves one file, xLamina-report-…zip, to the Desktop. It holds the logs of this session and the two before it, the PC's details (Windows, processor, memory, graphics cards and driver) and xLamina's settings. It holds no images, and the name of your user folder is replaced. Send that file with a few words on what you did and what you saw.
  • Help > Open the log folder shows the log files themselves: one per session, the last ten.
  • If xLamina closed by itself or was ended during a render, the next start says so and offers to save the report.
  • If the files could not be written (disk full, a file open in another program), the finished images are still shown: use Save as… to keep them somewhere else before the next render.
  • After "The graphics driver stopped responding", close xLamina and open it again before rendering.
